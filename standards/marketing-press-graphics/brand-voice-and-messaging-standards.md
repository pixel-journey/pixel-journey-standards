# Px Brand Voice & Messaging Standards

**Consistent Tone, Language, and Storytelling Across All Pixel Journey Communications**

*Part of the Pixel Journey Standards Codex — Marketing, Press & Graphics Pillar*

---

## Purpose

Pixel Journey has a distinct personality: serious Web3 engineering blended with joyful retro pixel culture, community focus, self-custody values, and a touch of playful meme energy. All written communications — X posts, GitBook docs, press releases, onboarding copy, error messages, marketing materials, and community updates — should consistently reflect this voice.

This document defines the brand voice and messaging standards to ensure coherence across all touchpoints.

---

## Core Brand Voice Attributes

**Pixel Journey’s voice is:**

- **Confident but approachable** — We know what we’re doing technically, but we explain it clearly and without arrogance.
- **Joyful & Playful (with restraint)** — We celebrate retro pixel culture and meme energy, but we don’t overdo it or become childish.
- **Educational & Generous** — We genuinely want to help people understand Web3, WAX, and our ecosystem. We share knowledge freely.
- **Community-first** — We speak *with* the community, not *at* them. We celebrate holders, contributors, and builders.
- **Trustworthy & Self-Custody Focused** — We take security and user sovereignty seriously and communicate that clearly.
- **Optimistic & Forward-Looking** — We’re excited about the future of Pixel Journey and Web3, while staying grounded and realistic.

**What we are not:**
- Overly corporate or dry
- Hype-heavy or “to the moon” crypto bro energy
- Condescending or overly technical without explanation
- Generic Web3 marketing speak

---

## Messaging Principles

### Clarity Over Cleverness
- Prioritize clear, scannable communication.
- Use simple language when explaining complex topics.
- Avoid unnecessary jargon — when technical terms are needed, explain them briefly.

### Educational Spirit
- Most content should have some educational value (even hype posts can teach something).
- Explain the “why” behind features and decisions when possible.
- Help users understand the broader WAX ecosystem and self-custody principles.

### Community & Belonging
- Use inclusive language (“we”, “our”, “together”).
- Celebrate community contributions, holders, and builders.
- Avoid insider-only language that excludes new people.

### Tone by Context

| Context                  | Tone Guidance                                      | Examples of Good Tone                     |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| X / Social (hype)        | Playful + confident, short & punchy                | “Another day, another pixel-perfect drop” |
| X / Social (educational) | Clear, generous, slightly playful                  | “Here’s how verifiable entropy actually works…” |
| GitBook / Docs           | Clear, structured, educational, slightly warm      | Professional but human                    |
| Error messages           | Helpful, calm, actionable (never blaming)          | “We couldn’t load your assets. Try again?” |
| Press / Media            | Confident, clear, slightly premium                 | Professional but still characterful       |
| Onboarding / UI copy     | Friendly, guiding, low-friction                    | Warm and supportive                       |

## Do’s and Don’ts

**Do:**
- Use active voice when possible.
- Be specific and concrete.
- Inject light retro pixel or meme references when they feel natural.
- Explain technical concepts accessibly.
- Celebrate the community and builders.

**Don’t:**
- Use excessive hype language (“revolutionary”, “game-changing”, “disruptive”) without substance.
- Write walls of text without clear structure.
- Use insider jargon without explanation.
- Be overly formal or cold.
- Copy generic Web3 marketing templates.

---

## Quality Bar

AAA-tier messaging in Px means:
- Every piece of communication feels like it comes from the same thoughtful, confident, slightly playful voice.
- Technical topics are made accessible without dumbing down.
- The retro pixel culture and community values shine through naturally.
- Readers feel informed, respected, and welcomed.
